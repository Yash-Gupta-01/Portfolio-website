# LinkedIn Outreach (referrals, intros, re-engagement)

The job of a LinkedIn message is to convert a stranger or a cold contact into one of three things: a referral, a 15-minute call, or a recruiter reply. It is not a cover letter. The reader is busy, skims on a phone, and decides in two seconds whether to ignore you. Every message here is built to survive that two-second skim.

## Who this is for (bake this in — do not invent a different persona)

Yash is an **early-career software engineer (~1 year)**, currently **Associate Software Engineer at ISchoolConnect** (Noida), graduated **B.Tech ECE, BPIT, 2025**. **Honesty (non-negotiable, same as his résumé):** employer is **ISchoolConnect** (never EDMO). His title is **Associate Software Engineer** — don't inflate to senior/lead. His CRM is **Slate** (not Salesforce). Angular is **Angular 14** (not AngularJS). Never claim a referral, a degree, or a tenure he doesn't have.

## Step 1 — Identify the intent

Ask (or infer from context) which of these the message is. The structure differs:

| Intent | When | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral — warm** | He has time, the person is an alum/ex-colleague, or the company rewards relationship-first | A short call, *then* a referral |
| **Referral — direct** | Role closes soon, or the person is a stranger who gets many asks | An immediate "will you refer me" |
| **Re-engagement** | He already connected with this person earlier and went quiet | Revive the thread with a concrete, relevant update |
| **Recruiter reply** | A recruiter messaged him, or he's replying to a job post's hiring contact | Signal fit fast, move to a screen |

If it's genuinely ambiguous, default to **referral — direct** for cold strangers (respects their time) and **warm** for alumni/ex-colleagues (the relationship is the asset).

## Step 2 — Respect the channel's hard limits

- **Connection-request note: ~300 characters.** This is the tightest and most common case. No room for a proof paragraph — one line of who he is, one line of the specific ask. Cut every filler word.
- **1st-degree message / InMail: longer**, but still keep it under ~120 words. A wall of text is an instant skip.
- **One ask per message.** "Refer me AND review my résumé AND tell me about the team" is three asks and gets zero replies.
- Lead with *their* relevance to *him*, not his life story. The first clause should make them think "this is about a role I can help with," not "another job seeker."

## Step 3 — Personalize with one real, specific hook

A referral DM works when it proves Yash did 30 seconds of homework. Use exactly one specific hook, drawn from real, verifiable info:
- Shared school (**BPIT**) or shared past employer (**ISchoolConnect**) — strongest, makes the alumni angle natural.
- The person's actual team/product, or a recent post of theirs.
- The specific open req (paste the Job ID / title), so the ask is concrete.

Never fake the hook ("I loved your post on X" when he didn't read it). A fabricated hook that gets caught is worse than no hook.

## Tone and etiquette

- Warm, direct, low-pressure. "Either way, thanks" outperforms a hard close — it signals he respects their time and isn't entitled to the favor.
- No flattery inflation, no "I'm your biggest fan." No emoji unless the recipient's own style invites it.
- Match his real seniority. An early-career engineer who writes like a confident peer (not a desperate applicant, not a faux-senior) reads as someone worth referring.
- Always offer to make the favor small: "I'll send a tailored résumé so it's 2 minutes for you."

## Output format

When asked for a message, produce the **single best-fit version** for the intent, ready to paste — not a menu of five. If two intents are genuinely plausible (e.g., warm vs. direct), give those two labeled variants and one line on when to pick each. Keep connection-request notes inside 300 characters and say the character count. Flag any place where Yash needs to drop in a real hook or pick his strongest role-relevant proof point.
